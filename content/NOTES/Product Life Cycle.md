*All products have a life cycle.*
**Development**: significant capital is  invested into the product to create a practical, usable good.
**Introduction**: The product is first released into the market.
**Growth**: Consumers grow aware of the product and sales increase
**Maturity**: The product reaches its full market, maximum sales are met here ( in the [[Boston Consulting Group Matrix]], this would be a cash cow)
**Decline**: Over time, the product loses favourability with the public, due to obsolescence, new and better products made by the company, or increased competition. Sales go down.

Some products have a long maturity (Coca Cola is still going strong despite being a cash cow for decades), while others have a short maturity (trendy, viral products tied to a short-term idea or timeframe often go out quick - think labubus, matcha, dubai chocolate).

The future of a product depends on what you decide at the beginning of the life cycle - do you intend for this to stay in the market for years upon years, or do you want to continuously improve and make iterations to the product (The newer iPhone phases out older ones - done deliberately).

Organizations need to be able to determine which stage of the life cycle their product is in.
