Differentiation, subset of [[Planning]], affects [[P.O.L.C]] in a different way. 
> [!example] Examples of Differentiated Competitors
> Saks Fifth Avenue, Ferrari, Apply

Differentiated competitors cannot stand out using prices, like a [[Cost Leadership]]-oriented business could. Instead, they must stand out using different methods. They can pass off high prices by making unique, novel, and appealing products. 
The main thing they say is: We may not be the cheapest, but we're the best.
Features of a Differentiated Competitor:
- They sacrifice efficiency for flexibility
- Prefer decentralization, decisions are made as low as possible in the chain of command[^1]
- Most important parts are [[Marketing]] and [[Customer Relations]], as most senior managers are from these areas and Design
- They need to hire creative, independent problem solvers
- Best example of a differentiated manager: Steven Jobs[^2]
- Control systems used to see: Market Share; Customer Satisfaction; how to add new products
- Brand Strength and Customer loyalty are very important for this kind of business
- They need new products to increase brand loyalty

SUMMARY: Differentiation is all about offering something unique to the public that customers perceive as valuable, justifying a high price

[^1]: Why do they want this? For a differentiated competitor, the market is not steady, as they can be upturned at any moment. They need to be on top of current events, and act proactively so that they continue to run. If all decisions are made up at high management, it takes a long time for them to receive information of changes and even longer to create a decision. For example, if a client wants a custom car, they don't escalate the situation super duper high to corporate. Instead, the regional manager will make the decision to do it or no, which makes the business more flexible and adept to change. 


[[Organizational Charts and Designs#Product Organization]] is the main thing they use. They also use [[Decentralized Organizations|a decentralized]] framework[^3].
