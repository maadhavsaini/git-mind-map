These can all be mapped on the [[Product Life Cycle]] btw:
If you have a lot of market share in a rapidly growing market, you are a Star! If you have low market share in a rapid market, you're a Question Mark?
If you have a lot of market hare in a slowly growing market, you're a Cash Cow. If you have little market share in a slow market, you're a a Dog

## Actions One Can Pursue Regarding the General Classification of One or Many Goods or Services Provided by The Business One Participates In
The [[Strategy]] you adopt...
### Feed the Stars
The stars are products you can hopefully convert into cash cows once the market stabilizes. Right now, however, you need to innovate and improve stars, since the market is still growing and new competitors could upset your position as the dominant market shareholder.
Do: give money, make advertisements and develop product
Money from Cash Cows will help you invest capital into Stars! Aim to get as much money as possible from Cash Cows.
### Milk the Cows
The best Cash Cows are the ones that make you happy. Since Cash Cows are already dominant, you don't need to feed it any more that it needs ([[Law of Diminishing Returns]] 👀). Adding more money won't increase customer base, so just do the bare minimum and get a lot of money from it.
### Shoot the Dogs
If you have low share in a slow market, it's not going to get any better. Dominance has already been asserted by some companies and their products, and the market is mature - customers are content and don't want you anymore. In this scenario, it's best to cut your losses and end the product line - you could sell the line, discontinue the product, etc
### Question Mark?
Right now, you have a low share in a fast market. At this moment, you can either:
1. Invest into the product and push it up to a star
2. Invest into the product but fail, since another product is more-liked
3. Call it quits early-on and shoot it like a dog.

> [!warning] YOUR ACTIONS HERE CHANGE DEPENDING ON IF YOU'RE A COST LEADER OR DIFFERENTIATED COMPETITOR
