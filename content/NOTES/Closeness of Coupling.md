Subset of [[Controlling]].
When controlling, it's important to see the intervals at which feedback is received. If too sparingly, the company/person can be wrong for a very long time before any corrections are made and course is set, which can often be very detrimental if left for too long. If feedback is given too much, participants may not get a chance to change their behaviour, also a sign of micromanagement. Set intervals at which feedback is given to the company, to individual employees, and of the like sort.
This is it. Closeness of Coupling.

Example: 360 degree performance: when you receive feedback from everyone you come into contact with at your job (superiors, coworkers, subordinates).
- if too loose, like once a year, it'll be months before they find out they are doing the wrong thing, and even more before they are fully set on the path needed to continue
- if too tight, like once a week, there will be too much feedback being given with not enough time to implement, and employees can feel distrusted and watched. Makes them nervous and uncomfortable.

Note: If you have to prepare something for a senior, make your coupling tighter than the senior's so that you have more iterations / checks before you hand off the prepared.
